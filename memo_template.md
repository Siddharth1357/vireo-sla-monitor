# Business Memo: Reducing First-Response SLA Breaches

**To:** Neha Kulkarni
**Subject:** Data-backed plan to reduce SLA breaches and policy-credit exposure
**Reporting period:** April–June 2026, using 12 complete weeks for comparison

## Executive Summary

The Vireo support-ticket analysis indicates that first-response SLA performance worsened in the latest comparable period. The breach rate increased from **24.35% in January–March 2026 to 25.53% in April–June 2026**. With hiring frozen, the immediate opportunity is to improve how existing support capacity is managed, using the weekly report to identify recurring patterns by channel, resolving agent, and historical shift.

## Findings

* **Latest-period baseline:** 498 breaches across 1,951 tickets, a 25.53% breach rate.
* **Comparison period:** 490 breaches across 2,012 tickets, a 24.35% breach rate.
* **Policy-credit exposure:** At ₹350 per breach, 498 breaches correspond to an estimated ₹1,74,300 in policy credits, assuming each breach attracts the stated credit and is resolved under the policy.
* **Operational visibility:** The tool groups breaches by week, resolving agent, historical shift, and channel, enabling targeted investigation without immediately proposing additional headcount.

## Proposed Goal for the Next 12 Weeks

Reduce the first-response SLA breach rate to **24.35% or lower**, returning to the rate observed in the comparison period.

At a comparable volume of 1,951 tickets, this would mean approximately 475 or fewer breaches. Preventing 23 breaches would correspond to an estimated **₹8,050 in avoided policy credits** (23 × ₹350).

This is a proposed target and scenario, not a guarantee of future performance or verified savings.

## Recommended Actions

1. **Review weekly exceptions:** Identify recurring breach patterns by channel, resolving agent, and shift; investigate operational causes before assigning responsibility.
2. **Prioritise channel-specific improvements:** Review chat and email workflows alongside voice and social queues, using the relevant policy response targets.
3. **Improve queue handling:** Review workload distribution, handoffs, and escalation practices within existing staffing.
4. **Track progress weekly:** Monitor breach rate, breach count, ticket volume, and estimated policy credits against the baseline.
5. **Validate financial impact:** Ask Finance to reconcile policy-based estimates with actual issued credits before reporting realised savings.

## Assumptions and Limitations

The comparison uses 12 complete Monday–Sunday weeks per period, rather than exact calendar-quarter totals. The results are therefore comparable-period estimates. The ₹350 amount is the policy credit per breach, not independently verified Finance expenditure. This analysis shows observed performance patterns; it does not establish why breaches occurred or prove that any particular factor caused the change. The proposed target should be reviewed after the first four weeks of monitoring.

**Recommendation:** Begin a 12-week operational improvement cycle using existing resources, review progress weekly, and report both SLA performance and Finance-validated credit outcomes.
